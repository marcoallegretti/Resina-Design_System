# Minimum interactive targets (candidate)

Every standalone interactive control MUST provide a hit region that fully contains an axis-aligned square at least as large as the resolved minimum below. The visual body MAY be smaller than the hit region. Component contracts MAY require a larger region, and the larger requirement wins. Adjacent hit regions MUST NOT overlap in a way that makes activation ambiguous. A rounded or clipped target does not satisfy the rule merely because its bounding box is large enough. Inline links within flowing text need a separate content contract and MAY use the WCAG inline exception.

The headless minimum is 24 by 24 logical units for every validated environment snapshot. If `inputCapabilities` contains `coarsePointer` or `directTouch`, the minimum is 48 by 48 logical units. Other capabilities do not lower it. Mixed input uses the larger requirement, even when `finePointer` is also available. Density, viewing profile, quality policy, renderer capability, and text scale MUST NOT reduce the hit region below this floor. No backend, toolkit, or device name participates in the decision.

The 24-unit floor follows [WCAG 2.2 Target Size (Minimum)](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/target-size-minimum), which uses 24 by 24 CSS pixels for pointer targets with defined exceptions. Resina chooses a minimum region rather than relying on those exceptions. The 48-unit coarse-input floor follows [Android's 48 dp touch-target guidance](https://developer.android.com/guide/topics/ui/accessibility/views/apps-views) and exceeds [Apple's general 44 pt button hit-region guidance](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/buttons). DTCG `px` is an idealized viewport pixel with native logical-unit equivalents; these values are not physical display pixels.

The [target vectors](../conformance/interaction/target-minimum-vectors.json) define the derived minimum for mixed capabilities and density or viewing-profile changes. This requirement does not prescribe a visual size, a component layout, or a hit-testing implementation. Backends remain responsible for placing the full region within usable geometry and ensuring that the actual interactive shape contains the required square.
