# Extruded surface contour (candidate, 0.1.0)

Blueprint §§21–22, 27–28, and 36 require directional depth that survives Tier 0. The [extruded contour request](../schemas/extruded-contour-request.schema.json) supplies a surface size, logical corner radii, explicit `layoutDirection`, and a physical `offset`, all under version `0.1.0`. Coordinates use logical `px`, with `x` rightward and `y` downward. Offset may come from [key-light resolution](34-key-light.md); the geometry operation does not infer lighting, elevation, a backend, or a material family.

The filled front shape `F` is a convex rounded rectangle at origin `(0,0)`. First normalize the input radii using the [bounded corner rule](09-geometry.md). Then map logical corners to physical corners: LTR uses topStart, topEnd, bottomEnd, bottomStart for top-left, top-right, bottom-right, bottom-left; RTL uses topEnd, topStart, bottomStart, bottomEnd. A corner with either normalized radius zero is square. Physical offset MUST NOT mirror with layout direction.

The silhouette is the sweep `S = {p + t × offset | p ∈ F, 0 ≤ t ≤ 1}`. Equivalently, it is the convex hull of the front and translated front shapes, or their [Minkowski sum with a line segment](https://doc.cgal.org/Manual/3.4/doc_html/cgal_manual/Minkowski_sum_2/Chapter_main.html). Merely painting the two endpoint shapes can leave gaps. For every physical direction `n`, its support function satisfies `hS(n) = hF(n) + max(0, dot(n, offset))`. This identity is independent conformance evidence for the silhouette. Side-plane fill can later use `S` beneath the front fill; the operation does not prescribe pigment, outlines, clipping, or paint order.

All input fields are required. Missing or unknown fields, unsupported versions, negative/nonfinite dimensions or radii, and nonfinite offsets MUST fail diagnostically without a result. Validate radii and offset even when dimensions collapse. A front shape with zero width or height is empty for this filled-area contract: its result has `bounds: null` and `segments: []`, regardless of finite offset. It MUST NOT create a filled plane by extruding a degenerate line. Otherwise bounds are `(min(0,offset.x), min(0,offset.y), width + abs(offset.x), height + abs(offset.y))`. Overflow of these dimensions or resolved coordinates MUST fail.

## Analytic contour record

The [result](../schemas/extruded-contour-result.schema.json) records finite physical bounds and a closed clockwise boundary using ordinary lines and axis-aligned elliptical arcs. It contains no toolkit path objects, tessellation, device scale, or GPU commands. A line has `from` and `to` physical points. An arc has a physical `center`, strictly positive `radii`, and unit radial parameters `start` and `end`. These parameters are **not physical endpoints**: an endpoint is `center + (radii.x × radial.x, radii.y × radial.y)`. Traverse clockwise in physical downward-y coordinates, through a positive angle no greater than one quarter turn. Every segment ends where the next begins, including the final-to-first join. Radial normalization, arc span, and connectivity are semantic checks beyond JSON Schema.

The canonical record visits top-left, top-right, bottom-right, and bottom-left corners, starting with the top-left arc when present. Visit each corner arc before its following straight edge. Within an elliptical corner, translate an arc portion if its outward normal has positive dot product with offset; otherwise leave it at the front. The outward normal is proportional to `(radii.y × radial.x, radii.x × radial.y)`. Split at a strict interior zero of that dot product, if present. Connect the two copies at a support switch with a line parallel to offset. Straight edges follow the same positive-dot rule; zero-dot edges remain at the front. Connect successive portions by bridge lines when their endpoints differ, and close the record. Omit zero-length straight and bridge lines; retain quarter-arc boundaries and collinear bridges rather than merging them. This canonical representation makes serialized results reproducible without requiring a renderer's own internal path representation to match it.

Computing a support switch MUST preserve the relative products `offset.x × radii.y` and `offset.y × radii.x` without overflow or simultaneous underflow. Separately normalizing the two input vectors is insufficient when opposite extreme components multiply. The reference decomposes finite factors into binary significands and exponents, scales the two products together, and computes their perpendicular unit direction with a bounded square root. Equivalent algorithms are permitted within the output tolerance.

Coordinates, radii, and bounds use absolute tolerance `1e-12` logical `px` or relative tolerance `1e-12`, whichever is larger. Unit radial components use absolute tolerance `1e-12` only. Record membership, segment kind/order/count, version, and null/empty structure remain exact for this resolver protocol. [Public vectors](../conformance/geometry/extruded-contour-vectors.json) cover square, elliptical and circular shapes, split curves, collinear bridges, normalized radii, RTL, collapsed shapes, numeric extremes, and failures. The reference additionally checks the support-function identity across directions and authored fallback shapes.

`resina-extruded-contour <path|->` reads one UTF-8 JSON request up to 1 MiB. Success exits 0 with one JSON result and no diagnostic; invalid input exits 1 with a diagnostic and no result; usage errors exit 2. Duplicate JSON members are rejected at every depth. The [external checker](../tools/check_extruded_contour_backend.py) validates the public protocol and repeated output independently of Rust.

The contour is available at Tier 0. Backends may realize arcs analytically or approximate them to their documented conformance quality; expensive lighting and shaders are unnecessary. This contract resolves silhouette geometry. Exterior edge bands, complete opaque surface IR, treatment geometry, state-specific appearance, advanced curvature, and renderer conformance remain separate work.
