# Icon content (candidate, schema 0.1.0)

An [icon content document](../schemas/icon-content.schema.json) describes one icon by Resina metadata. It does not contain artwork. Its `schemaVersion` is `0.1.0`. The `reference` is an opaque namespaced identity of the form `namespace:name`. The namespace is one or more dot-separated segments, each a lowercase ASCII letter followed by lowercase letters or digits. The name is one or more dash-separated segments of lowercase letters or digits, with no empty segment. Resina does not interpret the segments. A theme or backend icon mapping supplies the artwork for a reference, as a font mapping supplies faces for the [typography](11-typography.md) roles. That mapping, its lookup rules and its diagnostics are separate contracts. Core ships no icon catalogue.

`kind` is `symbolic` or `color`. A symbolic icon is a set of coverage masks, each bound to a semantic colour role. Its `layers` array is nonempty and lists the masks in paint order. The first layer has role `content`, which stands for the content colour role of the part that hosts the icon. Each further layer names a distinct role from `accent.primary`, `accent.secondary`, `accent.tertiary`, `status.error`, `status.warning`, `status.success` and `status.info`. A layer adds a declared meaning to the icon; it is not decoration. A colour icon is consumer artwork that Resina never recolours. It declares no layers. Its `bakedLight` member states whether the artwork contains light or shading. A symbolic icon declares no `bakedLight`.

`grid` is the positive integer edge of the square design grid, in design units with the origin at the top-left. `inkBounds` is the smallest rectangle that contains the icon's painted coverage. `opticalBounds` is the rectangle that a component aligns and centres in place of the grid, so a visually unbalanced shape can sit correctly. The two rectangles may differ in either direction. Each has a nonnegative `x` and `y` and a positive `width` and `height`, and lies within the grid: `x + width` and `y + height` do not exceed `grid`. Measurements may be fractional.

Every icon declares its `direction`; there is no default. `mirror` reflects the icon horizontally about the vertical centre line of the grid under right-to-left layout. Its bounds reflect with it. `invariant` keeps the same artwork in both directions. `directionalVariants` names an `ltr` and an `rtl` reference. The two are distinct, and neither repeats the icon's own reference. Material light is physical: the [key light](34-key-light.md) and any lighting a backend adds keep their direction when an icon mirrors. A colour icon with `bakedLight` `true` therefore MUST NOT declare `mirror`. It uses `invariant` or `directionalVariants`.

Missing, unknown or duplicate members, an unsupported version, an invalid reference, a nonintegral or nonpositive grid, bounds outside the grid or without positive extent, a direction without its required variants or with variants it does not take, and a layer list that breaks the rules above are errors. Duplicate JSON members MUST be rejected before conversion to an object tree. An invalid document is not published in part, and it is never replaced by a placeholder icon.

This contract does not define icon size roles, how an icon scales next to a label, the contrast an icon needs against its surface, mapping lookup or resource failure, rendering, or an icon-only Command. Those need their own contracts and evidence. The [content vectors](../conformance/content/icon-content-vectors.json) list valid and rejected documents. A case with `schemaValid` `true` and an `error` is valid against the schema but violates a rule the schema cannot express, such as bounds that exceed the grid.
