# Authored fixed themes (candidate)

The [light](../tokens/themes/light.json) and [dark](../tokens/themes/dark.json) sources are authored Resina themes, rather than illustrative conformance palettes. Each supplies every current material, color, spatial, and typography assignment, a Frost pigment strength, and an opaque color fallback for translucent chrome. Their DTCG Resolver Module 2025.10 documents compose the authoritative [foundation source](../tokens/foundation.json) through the explicit external URI `foundation.json` before theme pigments and typography tokens. Compilation MUST receive that URI mapped to the foundation's UTF-8 source text; no network or filesystem fetch is implied by the theme source.

The paired themes use a restrained warm mineral base and a deep blue-black base, with teal as the primary accent, blue as the secondary accent, and amber as the tertiary accent. Cast carries structural surfaces, Frost carries chrome, Elastomer carries interactive controls, and Gel is limited to transient surfaces and feedback. Their explicit role mappings preserve that hierarchy across light and dark conditions. The sources define semantic choices and do not supply a font file, component layout, edge, highlight, motion, or render-ready paint.

The Rust reference integration test compiles both themes against the authored foundation, resolves baseline, 200% text scale, and zero-capability high-contrast environments, checks selected role assignments and opaque chrome fallback, and verifies that every authored sRGB pigment's hex fallback matches its numeric components. As a palette preflight, it requires at least 4.5:1 opaque sRGB contrast for content and status colors against each authored opaque surface, primary content against selection, and inverse content against the three accents. It requires at least 3:1 for outline and focus against each authored opaque surface. These thresholds draw on [WCAG 2.2 text contrast](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/#contrast-minimum) and [non-text contrast](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/#non-text-contrast). They are authoring checks on named opaque pairs, not a claim of accessibility of rendered UI: a backend must still evaluate actual foreground and adjacent colors after material, treatment, state, transparency, and backdrop resolution as required by [the contrast contract](12-contrast.md).
